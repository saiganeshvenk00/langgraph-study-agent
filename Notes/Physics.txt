Physics is the study of matter, energy, space, and time, and the fundamental forces that govern them. It seeks to understand how the universe works at every scale, from subatomic particles to galaxies. Physics underpins all natural sciences and drives advances in technology and engineering.

Motion is described using quantities such as displacement, speed, velocity, and acceleration. Newton's three laws of motion form the foundation of classical mechanics. The first law states that an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net external force. The second law relates force, mass, and acceleration: F = ma. The third law states that every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

Energy exists in many forms including kinetic, potential, thermal, electrical, chemical, and nuclear energy. The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance, and power is the rate at which work is done.

Waves transfer energy without transferring matter. They can be transverse, like light waves, or longitudinal, like sound waves. Key wave properties include amplitude, wavelength, frequency, and speed. The electromagnetic spectrum includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays, all travelling at the speed of light in a vacuum.

Electricity and magnetism are closely related. Electric current is the flow of charge, and it is driven by voltage through a conductor with resistance, as described by Ohm's law: V = IR. Magnetic fields are produced by moving charges. Modern physics extends into quantum mechanics, which describes behaviour at atomic scales, and relativity, which addresses motion at speeds approaching the speed of light.
