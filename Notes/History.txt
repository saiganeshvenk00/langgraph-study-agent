History is the study of past events, societies, and human behaviour over time. Historians use primary sources such as documents, letters, and artefacts, as well as secondary sources, to reconstruct and interpret the past. Understanding history helps explain the origins of present-day societies, conflicts, and institutions.

The earliest civilisations emerged around 3500 BCE in Mesopotamia (modern Iraq) and the Nile Valley. These societies developed writing, legal codes, monumental architecture, and organised religion. The Code of Hammurabi in Babylon was one of the earliest written sets of laws. Ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome each made lasting contributions to governance, philosophy, art, and engineering that shaped Western civilisation.

The Middle Ages in Europe (roughly 500 to 1500 CE) were characterised by feudalism, the dominance of the Catholic Church, and the fragmentation of political power following the collapse of the Western Roman Empire. The Crusades, the Black Death, and the Magna Carta were defining events of this era. Meanwhile, Islamic scholars preserved and advanced knowledge in mathematics, astronomy, and medicine during Europe's medieval period.

The early modern period brought the Renaissance, the Reformation, and European exploration and colonisation. The Renaissance revived classical learning and produced extraordinary achievements in art and science. The Protestant Reformation challenged papal authority and reshaped European Christianity. European powers colonised vast territories across the Americas, Africa, and Asia, with profound and often devastating consequences for indigenous peoples.

Modern history from the 18th century onward includes the Age of Revolutions, industrialisation, imperialism, and two devastating world wars. The French and American Revolutions promoted ideals of liberty and democracy. The Industrial Revolution transformed economies and society. The First and Second World Wars reshaped global borders and led to the formation of the United Nations. The 20th century also saw decolonisation, the Cold War, and rapid technological advancement.
